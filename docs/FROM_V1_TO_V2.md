# From MammoDiffusion V1 to V2

## 1. Project continuity

MammoDiffusion V2 is not a separate project and does not replace the scientific objective of V1. It is the direct methodological and experimental evolution of the original MammoDiffusion pipeline. Both versions ask the same central question: whether class-conditioned synthetic mammograms generated by diffusion models can be useful for downstream breast-cancer classification, and how that strategy compares with real-only training and traditional augmentation.

The continuity is concrete. V1 and V2 use the RSNA breast-cancer dataset and the same canonical cohort of 2,916 patients: 486 positive and 2,430 negative cases. The patient-level splits are also preserved: 2,041 patients for training, 437 for validation, and 438 for testing, with 340, 73, and 73 positive cases respectively. Each patient contributes one selected 512×512 grayscale MLO image. Direct inspection of the canonical manifests confirms that the patient, image, and label keys are the same in both versions.

V1 established the initial end-to-end study: data preparation, two diffusion strategies, synthetic-data evaluation, downstream classification, comparison with traditional augmentation, and sustainability accounting. V2 retains those questions and experimental families while making the protocol more controlled, the evidence easier to reproduce, and the interpretation more statistically cautious. Its main contribution is therefore methodological strengthening rather than a change of topic or a claim that the earlier project lacked value.

## 2. What V1 established

V1 built the first complete MammoDiffusion prototype around the RSNA Screening Mammography Breast Cancer Detection data. Its preprocessing selected the MLO view, retained one image per patient, converted images to grayscale PNG, resized them to 512×512, normalized their visual orientation, and created patient-level train, validation, and test partitions. This provided a stable cohort on which both generative and discriminative experiments could be developed.

For conditional generation, V1 investigated two complementary approaches. The first fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1 on the mammography domain. The second trained a latent diffusion model from scratch, using a VAE and U-Net pipeline. V1 also compared the fine-tuned and from-scratch families, evaluated RAW and filtered generations, and reported established image-generation proxies including FID, Inception Score, precision, recall, density, and coverage.

The downstream study used an ImageNet-initialized ResNet-50 classifier. Its principal conditions included real-only training, training with real and synthetic images, and synthetic-only training. Additional experiments compared diffusion-based augmentation with traditional image augmentation. These components connected image generation to the practical question of whether synthetic samples could change classification performance on real mammograms.

Finally, V1 treated computational sustainability as part of the research question rather than as an afterthought. It collected energy and carbon information for relevant stages and compared traditional and diffusion-based augmentation workflows. Taken together, these elements made V1 a valuable first full-stack prototype: it linked preprocessing, generation, filtering, classification, evaluation, and sustainability in one coherent project.

## 3. Why V2 was developed

V2 was developed because a complete prototype and a strong confirmatory protocol serve different purposes. The V1 workflow demonstrated feasibility, but several limitations constrained the strength of general conclusions that could be drawn from it.

The main classifier evidence relied on a single seed and one principal architecture, ResNet-50. It did not use probability ensembles, so run-to-run variability could not be separated systematically from condition effects. Confidence intervals were not applied uniformly to the main comparisons, and there was no predefined correction for testing multiple hypotheses. Threshold selection and development-time consultation of test outputs were also less strictly separated than in V2. These points do not invalidate V1; they limit how confidently its observed differences can be generalized.

The original generative evaluation relied primarily on a generic Inception representation. That representation is useful for established image metrics, but it is not specialized for radiology. Duplicate and memorization controls were more limited, RAW and filtered pools were not always compared under one uniform benchmark, and some experimental comparisons differed in more than one factor at a time. Provenance, cache invalidation, resume behavior, and executable protocol checks were also less explicit than they are in V2.

V2 addresses these verifiable limitations by freezing the experimental matrix, separating selection from final testing, retaining keyed prediction artifacts, and applying the same statistical framework across predefined comparisons. It does not retroactively redefine V1 results. Instead, it asks the same questions under a protocol designed to make uncertainty, non-replication, and negative findings visible.

## 4. Methodological evolution

| Area | V1 | V2 |
|---|---|---|
| Classifier architectures | One principal ImageNet-initialized ResNet-50 | MaxViT-512 and Mammo-FM |
| Random seeds | Principal results based on seed 42 | Seeds 17, 42, and 73 |
| Training conditions | Real-only, real+synthetic, synthetic-only; additional traditional-augmentation comparisons | Four frozen conditions per architecture: real-only, real+traditional augmentation, real+G02 positives, and real+G07 positives |
| Run matrix | Primarily single-run condition comparisons | 2 architectures × 4 conditions × 3 seeds = 24 completed runs |
| Ensembles | None in the principal comparison | Eight ensembles using the mean probability across three seeds |
| Decision thresholds | Validation-derived thresholds, with less uniform separation across workflows | Thresholds selected on validation and applied unchanged to test |
| Uncertainty | Not reported systematically for all principal effects | Patient-level paired bootstrap with a fixed protocol |
| Multiple comparisons | No predefined family-wise correction | Holm correction across eight preregistered comparisons |
| Generative representation | Generic Inception features | RAD-DINO as the primary ranking representation; Inception retained as a secondary descriptor |
| Generative metrics | FID, IS, precision, recall, density, and coverage | KID, descriptive FID, PRDC, diversity, stability, technical validity, and similarity diagnostics |
| Synthetic pools | Pool sizes and comparison rules varied across analyses | Canonical 1,361-image pools, equalized for official ranking |
| RAW and FILTERED | Both studied, but not under one fully uniform selection protocol | Kept explicitly separate; FILTERED is the official ranking condition |
| Duplicate controls | Limited within-pool checks | Exact-content and perceptual duplicate diagnostics |
| Memorization controls | Limited | Train-reference nearest-neighbour, hash, SSIM, and perceptual-hash checks |
| Generator selection | Validation-based choices embedded in individual workflows | Formalized, validation-only selection of G02 and G07 by family |
| Test isolation | Development workflows could consult test-derived outputs | Generator selection uses train/validation only; classifier thresholds are validation-frozen before final test evaluation |
| Configuration | Primarily notebook-local parameters | Versioned classifier, generator, registry, and selection configurations |
| Provenance and execution safety | More dependent on notebook state and local paths | Keyed artifacts, local fingerprints, cache invalidation, checkpoint/resume guards, and explicit execution gates |
| Automatic tests | No comparable scientific contract suite | Model-free fixtures and static checks for protocol, thresholds, isolation, caching, and repository layout |
| Reproducibility | Complete prototype, but with more manual reconstruction | Saved predictions and reports can be checked or regenerated without repeating model inference |

The scale of V2 is not the contribution by itself. More runs are useful only because the design fixes what is compared. The two architectures test whether a finding transfers across representations. The three seeds expose training variability. Mean-probability ensembles reduce dependence on one run. Validation-frozen thresholds prevent the test set from becoming an operating-point optimizer. Patient-paired bootstrap preserves the relationship between predictions made on the same people, while Holm correction makes the family of eight primary comparisons explicit.

The generator benchmark follows the same principle. RAD-DINO provides a radiology-oriented feature space, although it is not a substitute for mammography-specific expert assessment. KID, PRDC, diversity, duplicate, and memorization outputs describe different properties rather than being collapsed into a single claim of realism. RAW and FILTERED variants remain separate, and the two selected generators have distinct roles: G02 represents the fine-tuned family and G07 the from-scratch family.

## 5. Interpretation of the V2 results

The canonical final-evaluation report identifies one favorable but non-conclusive signal. For MaxViT-512, the real-only ensemble has a test PR-AUC of 0.4128, while the condition using positive samples from G07 has a PR-AUC of 0.5230. In the predefined patient-level paired bootstrap on the held-out test cohort, the mean difference for G07 minus real-only is +0.1069, with a 95% percentile interval from +0.0256 to +0.1852. The raw two-sided p-value is 0.010.

That raw result must be interpreted together with the predefined family of eight comparisons. After Holm correction, the adjusted p-value is 0.080, and the null hypothesis is not rejected at the 0.05 level. It is therefore appropriate to describe the result as a promising MaxViT-specific signal, not as a demonstrated improvement.

Mammo-FM does not replicate the benefit. Its real-only ensemble has a test PR-AUC of 0.3241, compared with 0.3161 for the G07 condition. The paired bootstrap mean difference is −0.0082, with a 95% interval from −0.0527 to +0.0345; the raw p-value is 0.702 and the Holm-adjusted value is 1.000. The interval includes both modest benefit and modest harm, while the point estimate is slightly negative.

The contrast between architectures is scientifically informative. It suggests that downstream utility depends on the interaction among generator, synthetic pool, classifier representation, and training dynamics, although V2 does not report a formal architecture-by-condition interaction test. The results do not support a general statement that synthetic data always improve classification. Non-replication and null or negative effects are not failed experiments: they constrain the claim and show why conclusions based on one classifier would be too broad.

## 6. Why V1 and V2 scores are not direct replacements

The shared cohort creates continuity, but it does not make aggregate V1 and V2 scores interchangeable. The classifier architecture changes from ResNet-50 to MaxViT-512 and Mammo-FM. V2 uses three seeds and probability ensembles rather than one principal run. Its condition definitions, frozen threshold policy, validation/test separation, and multiplicity-aware statistical analysis are also different. PR-AUC itself is threshold-independent; frozen thresholds govern operating-point metrics and test discipline, not the numerical PR-AUC difference.

Even where both versions use MLO grayscale 512×512 images, preprocessing implementation details and architecture-specific input transformations are part of the model protocol. The generator benchmark also changes from a primarily Inception-based evaluation to a RAD-DINO-first, equalized, RAW/FILTERED framework with broader validity checks. A numerical difference between a V1 metric and a V2 metric could therefore reflect architecture, training, threshold, representation, pool construction, or evaluation policy rather than a single improvement in the underlying method.

V2 should consequently be read as a more rigorous re-evaluation of the same scientific question, not as a simple numerical upgrade over V1. Within-version, same-architecture paired comparisons are the appropriate basis for interpreting condition effects.

## 7. Remaining dataset and validation limits

Methodological hardening cannot remove limitations that are intrinsic to the available cohort. The training split contains only 340 positive cases, and validation and test contain 73 positive cases each. The corresponding negative counts are much larger, so class imbalance remains substantial. Bootstrap intervals quantify sampling variation within this cohort, but they do not create additional positive cases or eliminate dataset-selection bias.

The image representation is also restricted. Only the MLO view is retained; the CC view is absent. There is one selected image per patient, at 512×512 resolution, so the study cannot assess bilateral or multiview consistency and may omit fine detail visible at higher resolution. The canonical target is a global binary patient/image label. The manifests provide no lesion bounding boxes, segmentations, lesion position, lesion size, morphology, or histology. Consequently, they do not establish where a malignant finding should appear in a generated image.

These constraints separate several endpoints that must not be conflated:

- **Global realism** asks whether generated images resemble the reference distribution under visual or feature-based measures.
- **Downstream utility** asks whether adding synthetic samples changes a classifier trained and evaluated on real data.
- **Class adherence** asks whether an image requested as positive or negative actually expresses the requested condition.
- **Radiological validity** requires qualified assessment of anatomy, acquisition plausibility, and lesion appearance.
- **Clinical validity** requires evidence that a system is safe, effective, and generalizable in an intended clinical setting.

MammoDiffusion V2 evaluates the first two endpoints more rigorously than V1, but it does not establish the latter three. There is no external-domain validation and no blinded radiologist evaluation. The global labels cannot prove that a generated positive image contains a valid lesion or that a generated negative image is free of suspicious findings. A generator may instead learn anatomy, acquisition style, prevalence-associated cues, preprocessing artifacts, or classifier-sensitive shortcuts.

For the same reasons, clinical generalization is not demonstrated. Results from this selected RSNA-derived cohort cannot be assumed to transfer to other institutions, devices, acquisition protocols, populations, views, or diagnostic workflows. RAD-DINO metrics and downstream classifier agreement remain proxies; neither is a clinical endpoint.

## 8. Final interpretation

MammoDiffusion V2 does not replace V1 or change its scientific objective. It turns the original experimental pipeline into a more controlled, reproducible, and statistically cautious study. V1 remains the foundation that established the end-to-end workflow and the project’s core research questions. V2 clarifies which observations persist under multiple seeds, independent classifier architectures, validation-frozen thresholds, paired uncertainty, and correction for multiple comparisons.

Its clearest contribution is methodological. The observed downstream benefit of synthetic data is architecture-dependent and remains promising rather than conclusive. The positive G07 signal on MaxViT warrants cautious interpretation precisely because it is not reproduced by Mammo-FM and does not cross the 0.05 threshold after Holm correction. That combination of a favorable signal, a failed replication, and explicit remaining limits is the scientifically appropriate conclusion of the V1-to-V2 evolution.
